Basics

Perimenopause, explained

What's actually changing hormonally, how it differs from menopause itself, and why one blood test on one day rarely tells the full story.

4 min read

Estrogen and progesterone stop declining smoothly and start swinging instead, which is why symptoms can feel different week to week. It's a transition, not a single event, and it typically runs for years before periods stop for good.

Typical length: 4–8 yrs Usually starts: 40s Ends: 12 mo after last period
Cycle

Why your cycle is often the first thing to change

Shorter cycles, longer cycles, or ones that skip a beat entirely — what "irregular" actually means, and when it's worth a closer look.

5 min read

Cycle length is often the earliest signal, before any symptom shows up. A cycle that's suddenly 10 days shorter or longer, or an occasional skipped one, usually reflects changing ovulation patterns rather than a problem on its own.

Short cycle: <21 days Long cycle: >35 days Track cycle to cycle, not just one
Symptoms

Hot flashes and night sweats, decoded

The falling-estrogen mechanism behind that sudden wave of heat, and what tends to make it better or worse.

3 min read

As estrogen drops, the body's internal thermostat narrows its comfort zone, so small temperature shifts can trigger a flush response. Caffeine, alcohol, and stress don't cause it, but they can make episodes more frequent.

Common trigger: caffeine Often worse: evening/night Usually lasts: under 5 min
Symptoms

The fatigue that doesn't match your sleep

Why exhaustion can show up before any other symptom does, and how to tell it apart from an ordinary bad week.

4 min read

This kind of tiredness can show up even after a full night's sleep, because fluctuating progesterone affects sleep quality, not just how many hours you get. For many people it's one of the first symptoms noticed, sometimes before any cycle change.

Often an early symptom Linked to sleep quality, not hours Frequently overlaps with brain fog
Symptoms

Brain fog, joint aches, and the symptoms nobody warns you about

The lesser-known list — and an honest answer on how confidently each one can be linked to hormones today.

5 min read

Word-finding trouble, achy joints, itchy or dry skin: these are real and hormonally plausible, but the evidence linking each one directly to estrogen is weaker than it is for hot flashes or sleep. We'll always say how confident we are, not just that a link exists.

Evidence: association, not proof Most common: brain fog, joint aches
Testing

When to test, and what a "normal" result doesn't rule out

Why a single lab visit can miss a fluctuating process, and what changes when you test over time instead.

4 min read

Hormones fluctuate hour to hour and cycle to cycle during perimenopause, so a single lab draw can land on a "normal" day and miss the pattern entirely. Testing once a week over several months is what actually reveals a trend.

One test: a single data point A pattern needs: 3+ cycles
Common questions
What is Inito Perimenopause?

An at-home hormone test paired with a daily symptom journal. You test your urine once a week using the same reader as our fertility product, log how you're feeling, and the app shows you the pattern between the two: what's likely connected and what isn't.

Who is this for?

Women in early to mid perimenopause who are still getting periods, even irregular ones. If your main goal is fast symptom relief, or you've already gone through menopause, this isn't the right fit yet; a doctor or hormone therapy provider will serve you better today.

What can I track?

Hot flashes, sleep, and mood are the core three, plus optional brain fog and joint aches. Your hormone readings (estrogen, LH, FSH, and progesterone) are measured automatically every time you test.

How often do I need to test?

Once a week, year round. We're watching for slower, longer-term patterns instead of a single fertile window.

What if my results don't show a clear pattern?

That's common. Perimenopause hormones are naturally erratic, so "not enough data yet" or "no clear link found" are honest answers you'll sometimes see, not a failure of the product.

Can perimenopause start in my 30s?

For some women, yes. Most people first notice symptoms in their mid 40s to mid 50s, but the transition can begin earlier. It typically lasts four to eight years, sometimes longer, before periods stop for good.

Will this diagnose me or replace my doctor?

No. It's an insight tool, not a diagnosis. Every insight comes with its confidence level built in, and the app builds a one page summary you can bring to an actual appointment.

Can I use this alongside hormone therapy (HRT)?

Not yet. Version one is built for women who aren't on hormone therapy, since HRT changes your hormone readings in ways the current pattern detection isn't tuned for. It's on our list for a future version.

Do I need a wearable like an Apple Watch or Whoop?

Not for version one. Everything here runs on your hormone test and your own symptom log. Pairing with a wearable for sleep and heart rate context is something we're exploring for a future version, not required today.

How is my data handled?

Your hormone readings and symptom logs are yours. We hold this data to the same rigorous privacy and security standard across every Inito product, and it's never used to make a medical decision without you.
